# ChurnCast requirements

> **Phase 1 deliverable** (reference version). Agreed with the Head of Growth on 2026-06-03, before any modelling.

## 1. The decision
At the end of every month the retention team calls **10 % of active customers** and may offer a
discount (cost about ₹200 per call and offer). Today they choose with five risk flags (the rule of thumb). We decide
**whom to call**, ranked by risk, and finance sizes the monthly retention budget from the predicted probabilities.
Calling a customer who would have stayed costs one call and a discount (₹200); not calling a customer who leaves
costs that customer's future revenue (three months of a Standard plan, about ₹9,900). Missing a churner is
therefore much more expensive than a wasted call, but the call capacity is fixed: what matters is how many churners
are in the top 10 %, not a yes/no threshold.

## 2. Stakeholders
| Who | Needs | What could go wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Head of Growth (sponsor) | more churners on the call list than the rule of thumb finds; a monthly number they trust | a model that looks better only on paper (leakage, random split) |
| Retention agents | a short, honest reason per customer ("skipped 70 % of meals last month") | reasons they cannot act on, or an AI note that invents facts |
| Finance | calibrated probabilities to size the budget | an over-confident model doubles the budget estimate |
| Data / engineering | a reproducible pipeline and an artefact the API can serve | a model nobody can rebuild, or one that breaks on the next export |
| Customers | not to be treated differently for who they are (age) | a call list that systematically misses one age group |

## 3. The prediction problem
- **Unit:** one row = one active customer at one month-end snapshot (active = a paid charge for month M).
- **Snapshot and cut-off:** the prediction is made at the end of month M; everything used must be known before
  the first instant of M + 1.
- **Label:** `churned = 1` when the customer has no paid charge for month M + 1. Refunds and failed payments are not
  renewals. The last month of the export has no label.
- **Horizon:** one month.
- **Allowed:** invoices, usage and tickets up to month M; signup data. **Not allowed:** anything from M + 1,
  `cancellation_reason` (known only after a customer leaves), and `age_band` as a feature (a protected attribute;
  used only to check fairness).

## 4. Success criteria (agreed BEFORE modelling)
- Ranking: out-of-time test **AUC ≥ 0.72** and at least **+0.03 above the rule of thumb** (B1).
- Calibration: **ECE ≤ 0.03**, and a Brier score below the prior (B0).
- The call list: precision, recall and lift of the top 10 % per month, reported against B1, with a monthly net
  value under an explicit assumption (25 % of contacted would-be churners stay; to be tested).
- Fairness: recall gap of the call list across age bands **≤ 0.15**.
- No leakage: no single feature with a training AUC ≥ 0.90.
- These are the six checks of the release gate (`contract/data-contract.yaml`, `gate`).

## 5. Data and its limits
The export (`data/raw/2026-05`) has customers, invoices, product usage and support tickets for January 2025 to
May 2026. It is **synthetic** (generated by `scripts/generate_data.py`, realistic but not real customers). It cannot
tell us whether a call *causes* a customer to stay (no experiment yet), covers 17 months of one business in five
Indian cities, and has known defects: two date formats, duplicated rows, prices as text with `₹`, negative usage
counts, orphan records, tickets resolved before they were created, blank channels and age bands.

## 6. Acceptance criteria for the pipeline
1. One command rebuilds every output from a clean checkout; the same input gives byte-identical features.
2. Every cleaning rule is applied in the contract's order and counted in `data_quality.issues`.
3. A failing `error` check stops the run with exit code 3; a `warn` is reported and the run continues.
4. No feature uses information from after the snapshot cut-off (a test appends future rows and expects the
   past features unchanged).
5. Training and test are separated in time; cross-validation folds are grouped by customer.
6. Python, SQL and R produce the same features (1e-9) and metrics and models (1e-6).
7. `scripts/eval_gate.py` blocks a model that fails any of the six gate checks.
8. The model artefact validates against `contract/model.schema.json` and is servable by the API.
9. Every explanation shown to an agent cites only facts computed from the model; an AI note that fails a
   guardrail is replaced by a deterministic template.

## 7. Risks and how the plan handles them
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Leakage (future rows, `cancellation_reason`) | point-in-time features, the future-rows test, the leakage scan and probe |
| A random split hides drift and overstates performance | out-of-time test months, grouped folds |
| Over-confident probabilities mislead finance | calibration in the gate (ECE, Brier vs prior) |
| An unfair call list | age band excluded from the features, recall gap in the gate |
| The rule of thumb is better than expected | B1 is a baseline every model must beat by 0.03 AUC |
| An AI explanation invents a number or a certainty | numbered facts, guardrails, template fallback |
| The next export changes (new values, formats) | a versioned data contract that stops the run (Phase 7) |

## 8. Out of scope (v1)
Uplift modelling (who is *persuadable*), choosing the discount, real-time scoring, customer-level automation
without an agent, and any model that uses protected attributes.
